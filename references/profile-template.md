# Profile template

Copy this to `profile.md` in your working folder (or `~/.jd-fit/profile.md`) and fill it in. Keep it short. Evidence matters more than adjectives.

```markdown
# Profile

## One line
<Who you are and what you want, in one sentence. Example: "Recent data science graduate looking for analyst roles in e-commerce.">

## Evidence (what I have actually done)
- <Skill or capability>: <what I did, with a number or an output>
- <Skill or capability>: <what I did, with a number or an output>
- <Skill or capability>: <what I did, with a number or an output>

## Target roles
- Main: <role family 1>, <role family 2>
- Also fine: <role family 3>
- Not interested: <work you want to avoid, such as pure sales calling or on-call engineering>

## Hard gates
- Location: <cities or remote>
- Work authorization: <what you have, or "need sponsorship">
- Languages: <language and level>
- Experience: <years you have; the most years you will accept in a posting>

## Preferences (used only to order similar-fit postings)
- Company type: <for example global company, startup>
- Anything else that is a tie-breaker, not a requirement

## Keywords (used by scripts/prefilter.py)
- High: <words that strongly signal a match, comma separated>
- Medium: <words that weakly signal a match>
- Negative: <words that signal a poor match, such as senior, principal, DevOps>
```

Tips:
- Three to six evidence lines are enough. Each should be something you could explain in an interview.
- Do not list a skill you only read about. The screener treats the profile as true and will score you on it.
- Keep preferences separate from requirements. A preferred company type should not make a role look like a better skill match.
